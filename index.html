What is HanCue?

New letters can feel like a whole new world. HanCue puts a familiar little guide above the Hangul, helping you find your way into Korean, one syllable at a time.

The cues are part of the font itself. Your text stays Korean when you type, copy, and share it.

HanCue adds Latin reading cues on top of Nanum Myeongjo, Nanum Gothic, and Nanum Pen Script.

How it works

Each Hangul syllable has its own Latin cue built into the font. Read along one block at a time, like 한국어 → han · guk · eo.

Getting to know the reading cues +

The cues use Revised Romanization letter values: eo, eu, and ui are Korean reading conventions, rather than English phonetic spellings. Each syllable is shown independently. Pronunciation in connected speech can differ from these cues.

All three typefaces cover 11,172 modern Hangul syllables. Standalone jamo and historical Hangul have no cues.

The typefaces beneath the cues

HanCue builds its Latin reading cues on top of three existing Nanum typefaces. The original letterforms are preserved; the annotated versions are distributed as HanCue Serif, HanCue Sans, and HanCue Script.
